## Completed: Electron configurations
Demonstrated:
- Shell = principal energy level, not literal path.
- Subshells s, p, d, f; orbitals are quantum states with spatial probability distributions.
- shell → subshell → orbital → electron.
- Orbital counts s=1, p=3, d=5, f=7; capacities 2, 6, 10, 14.
- Shell capacities 2, 8, 18, 32 and 2n² pattern.
- Aufbau: lowest-energy available orbitals fill first.
- Madelung n+l rule; ties resolved by lower n.
- Hund: equal-energy orbitals singly before pairing.
- Pauli: maximum two electrons/orbital; paired electrons have opposite spins.
- Can construct configurations and orbital diagrams, including sulfur, potassium and scandium.
- Noble-gas notation understood.
- 4s fills before 3d in the introductory sequence; period lengths and s/p/d/f block widths can be derived from filling order and subshell capacities.
- Can infer period, block and main-group valence-electron pattern from configurations and reverse the reasoning.
- Understands that n+l/Aufbau is a useful predictive model, not an immutable energy ladder.
- Once 3d is occupied, 4s electrons are generally removed first when forming positive ions; correctly derived Fe²⁺ = [Ar]3d⁶.
- Chromium and copper recognised as common exceptions: simple prediction versus lower-energy observed configurations.
- Transition-metal group numbering and detailed valence-electron treatment were not taught and are deliberately deferred rather than treated as required knowledge.

## Terminology to reinforce
- electrostatic attraction, not magnetic pull/nuclear force.
- strong nuclear force is inside the nucleus.
- orbital, not orbit.
- distinguish subshell from individual orbitals.
- groups = columns; periods = rows.
- valence electrons, not “valency electrons”.
- “differentiating electron” means the electron added relative to the preceding element.
