# Chemistry Study Record

## Purpose

This is the living checkpoint for the Chemistry Study project. Use it to
resume lessons without relying on the full chat history.

## Starting point

Returning to chemistry after not studying it since high school. Teaching
approach: rebuild from first principles, use conceptual reasoning before
memorisation, and regularly test understanding with short questions.

## Completed: Foundations of atomic structure

Demonstrated understanding of: - Atomic number, mass number, protons,
neutrons, electrons, isotopes and ions. - Neutral atoms have equal
numbers of protons and electrons. - The simple 2,8,8 shell model is
useful but incomplete. - Valence electrons are electrons in the outer
occupied shell.

## Completed: Introductory bonding

Demonstrated understanding of: - Ionic bonding as electron transfer
followed by electrostatic attraction. - Covalent bonding as electron
sharing. - The octet rule as a useful introductory model, not a
universal rule. - Electronegativity, bond polarity and the continuum of
covalent/ionic character.

## Completed: Periodic table and periodic trends

Demonstrated understanding of: - Groups are columns; periods are rows. -
Main-group position relates to valence-electron structure. - Shielding
and increasing nuclear charge. - Atomic-radius, first-ionisation-energy
and electronegativity trends. - Can derive trends using nuclear charge,
distance and shielding rather than memorising arrows.

## In progress: Electron configurations

Demonstrated understanding of: - A shell is a principal energy level,
not a literal electron path. - Subshells: s, p, d and f. - Orbitals are
quantum states with spatial probability distributions; electrons do not
travel around fixed orbital paths. - Hierarchy: shell → subshell →
orbital → electron. - Orbital counts: s = 1, p = 3, d = 5, f = 7. - Each
orbital holds a maximum of 2 electrons; subshell capacities are 2, 6, 10
and 14. - Shell capacities 2, 8, 18, 32 (2n²), explaining why 2,8,8 is
incomplete. - p orbitals differ in spatial orientation. - Aufbau
principle: lowest-energy available orbitals fill first. - 4s fills
before 3d in the introductory Aufbau sequence. - Madelung (n + l) rule:
lower n + l fills first; ties go to lower n. - Correctly applied n + l
to examples including 4p vs 5s and 4d/5p/6s/4f. - Hund's rule:
equal-energy orbitals fill singly before pairing. - Pauli: maximum two
electrons per orbital; paired electrons have opposite spins. - Correct
orbital diagrams for p⁴ and d⁶. - Neutral nitrogen (Z = 7) correctly
derived as 1s² 2s² 2p³.

## Terminology to reinforce

-   Use **electrostatic attraction**, not "magnetic pull" or "nuclear
    force", for nucleus--electron attraction.
-   Strong nuclear force concerns particles in the nucleus.
-   Use **orbital**, not "orbit".
-   Distinguish a **subshell** (e.g. 2p) from individual orbitals within
    it.
-   Groups = columns; periods = rows.
-   Use "valence electrons", not "valency electrons".

## Current conceptual level

The simple shell model has been successfully upgraded to the shell →
subshell → orbital picture. The learner can reason through orbital
capacities and the usual filling order rather than relying on memorised
sequences, and can apply Aufbau/Madelung, Hund and Pauli to introductory
examples.

Do not yet assume fluency with long electron configurations or
periodic-table blocks; these are the next consolidation step.

## Resume here

### Next lesson: Practising electron configurations, then mapping them onto the periodic table

Start with a short retrieval question using an element beyond nitrogen.
Ask the learner to determine the electron count and construct the
complete electron configuration, explaining the filling order rather
than simply recalling it.

Then: 1. Practise several complete configurations, including one
reaching 3p and then 4s. 2. Use orbital diagrams where useful to
reinforce Hund and Pauli. 3. Connect the highest occupied
subshell/configuration pattern to the s, p, d and f blocks. 4. Show how
periods and main-group positions emerge from electron configurations. 5.
Reconnect configurations to valence electrons and earlier periodic
trends. 6. Once the general pattern is secure, mention transition-metal
exceptions and the 4s/3d occupancy nuance.

Maintain one-question-at-a-time pacing and ask for reasoning, not just
final configurations.
